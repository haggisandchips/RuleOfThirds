Rule of Thirds: About

Why I Built This

A few years ago I took a photography course, and one of the first things we covered was composition, including the Rule of Thirds. We got an exercise built around it: look at a scene, imagine it split into a 3x3 grid, and take a photograph placing the interesting parts along those lines and on the points of intersection instead of dead-centre.

Simple idea, surprisingly hard to see without practice.

I'm a developer by trade, so naturally my solution was to enlist the help of technology rather than artistic skill. I put together a small Chrome extension that overlays that 3x3 grid directly on any image on any webpage - this helped me critique my own efforts and those of others on the course.

It helped me get the hang of it, and it turned out to help a few others on the course too, once I shared it around. That was the sign it was worth developing further.

It's grown a little since then - I've added the Phi Grid and Fibonacci Spiral; resizable overlays for framing a specific part of an image; and I've put effort into making it work on lots of sites1.

It's a free tool built to help people check their compositions. It's always been free, and it always will be free. 100%. No 'Pro' tier. No paywall of any kind.

If you want a new feature ... ask, and if I agree it fits with the spirit of the extension, then I will do my best to add it.

If it's useful to you, that's awesome because that's the point.

1. Probably the hardest bit to achieve.
