Rule of Thirds: About

Why I Built This

A few years ago I took a photography course, and one of the first things we covered was composition - specifically, the Rule of Thirds. We got an exercise built around it: look at a scene, imagine it split into a 3x3 grid, and place the interesting parts along those lines instead of dead-centre. Simple idea, surprisingly hard to see without practice.

I'm a developer by trade, so naturally my solution was to build something rather than just practice. I put together a small Chrome extension that overlays that 3x3 grid directly on any image on any webpage - so instead of imagining the lines, you can just look.

It helped me get the hang of it, and it turned out to help a few others on the course too, once I shared it around. That was the real signal it was worth keeping going: it's grown since then - a Phi Grid, a Fibonacci Spiral, resizable overlays for framing a specific part of an image - but the goal hasn't changed. It's a free tool built to help people actually see composition, not just read about it.
